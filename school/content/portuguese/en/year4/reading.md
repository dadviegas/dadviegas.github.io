# Reading & Writing

Reading and writing are the two most important skills in the Portuguese language curriculum. Strong readers understand more in every subject; strong writers can express their ideas clearly and persuasively.

---

## Reading Strategies

Good readers use strategies **before**, **during**, and **after** reading.

| When | Strategy | What to do |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Before | Preview | Look at the title, images, and headings. What will the text be about? |
| During | Highlight | Mark key ideas and unknown words. |
| During | Re-read | If a passage is confusing, read it again more slowly. |
| After | Summarise | Retell the main ideas in your own words. |
| After | Infer | What did the author imply but not directly state? |

> [!TIP]
> When you meet an unknown word, try to work out its meaning from the surrounding context before reaching for a dictionary.

---

## Types of Reading

- **Silent reading** — reading to yourself, focusing on meaning and comprehension.
- **Reading aloud** — reading for an audience; pay attention to pronunciation, intonation, and rhythm.
- **Expressive reading** — adjusting your voice to suit the mood of the text (suspense, joy, sadness).
- **Selective reading** — scanning for specific information (e.g., looking up a word in an index).

---

## Text Comprehension

When answering questions about a text, information falls into three categories:

1. **Explicit information** — written directly in the text. *"Pedro went to school."*
2. **Implicit information** — must be inferred; not stated but suggested. *"Pedro picked up his school bag"* → he is going to school.
3. **Personal opinion** — your own view, supported by evidence from the text.

> [!NOTE]
> In written answers, always begin by restating the question and quote from the text to support your response.

---

## The Writing Process

Writing a good text involves **four stages**:

```
1. Plan   →   2. Draft   →   3. Revise   →   4. Edit
```

### 1. Plan
- Decide on the topic, audience, and purpose.
- Brainstorm ideas or draw a mind map.

### 2. Draft
- Turn your plan into sentences and paragraphs.
- Don't worry about perfection — get your ideas down first.

### 3. Revise
- Read your text aloud. Does it make sense?
- Check: logical flow, vocabulary variety, punctuation, spelling.

### 4. Edit
- Make final corrections and produce a clean copy.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Every well-written text has an **introduction**, a **body** (development), and a **conclusion**. Never skip any part!

---

## Paragraph Structure

Each paragraph should contain:
- A **topic sentence** — states the main idea.
- **Supporting sentences** — explain or give examples.
- A **closing sentence** — wraps up the idea.

## Try it! 🎯

**1.** Read the excerpt and identify **one piece of explicit information** and **one piece of implicit information**:
> *"Ana arrived home soaking wet. She dropped her bag by the door and went straight to find a towel."*

**2.** Practice the **preview** strategy: look at the title *"The Enchanted Forest"* — write three questions you expect the text to answer.

**3.** Plan a 3-paragraph text about **your favourite animal**. Create a simple outline: introduction, two facts, conclusion.

**4.** Rewrite this sentence to make it more vivid and expressive: *"The dog ran around the garden."*
