# Text Types — Year 2

## Narrative Text (Texto Narrativo) — Stories

A **narrative text** tells a story with characters, a setting, and a sequence of events.

**Structure of a story:**

| Part | Portuguese | What it includes |
|------|------------|-----------------|
| Beginning | *Início* | Who? Where? When? — introduce characters and setting |
| Middle | *Desenvolvimento* | What happens? — events and problems |
| End | *Conclusão* | How does it end? — resolution |

**Common story openers in Portuguese:**
- *Era uma vez…* — Once upon a time…
- *Num dia…* — One day…
- *Há muito tempo…* — A long time ago…

> [!TIP]
> Good stories use **time connectors**: *primeiro* (first), *depois* (then), *entretanto* (meanwhile), *por fim* (finally), *de repente* (suddenly).

## Instructional Text (Texto Instrucional) — Recipes & Rules

An **instructional text** teaches you how to do something, step by step.

**Structure:**
1. **Title** — what you are making or doing
2. **Ingredients / Materials** (*ingredientes / material*) — what you need
3. **Steps** (*passos*) — numbered instructions, using verbs in the infinitive or imperative

**Example — Lemonade Recipe (Receita de Limonada):**

> **Ingredientes:** 2 limões, água, açúcar.
> **Preparação:**
> 1. Espremer os limões. *(Squeeze the lemons.)*
> 2. Misturar o sumo com água e açúcar. *(Mix the juice with water and sugar.)*
> 3. Servir com gelo. *(Serve with ice.)*

> [!NOTE]
> The **order** of steps in an instructional text is very important — don't mix them up!

## Simple Poetry (Poesia Simples)

**Poetry** (*poesia*) uses language in a creative way, often with **rhythm** and **rhyme**.

**Key poetry terms:**

| English term | Portuguese | Meaning |
|-------------|------------|---------|
| line | *verso* | each line of a poem |
| stanza | *estrofe* | a group of lines |
| rhyme | *rima* | lines that end with the same sound |
| rhythm | *ritmo* | the musical beat of the poem |

**Example poem:**
> *Borboleta colorida,*
> *voa livre e destemida.*
> *Pelas flores tu passas,*
> *com as tuas lindas asas.*

> [!TIP]
> Try counting the syllables in each line — poems often have the same number of syllables per line, which creates rhythm!
