# History of Portugal

Portugal has a rich and fascinating history! Our country was born over 800 years ago, and the Portuguese sailed to every ocean on Earth.

---

## The Founding of Portugal

### Before Portugal

Before Portugal existed, the Iberian Peninsula was home to many different peoples:

- **Lusitanians** — a warrior people who resisted the Romans (their leader was Viriatus)
- **Romans** — dominated the Peninsula for several centuries
- **Visigoths** — a Germanic people who came after the Romans
- **Moors** — Muslim people from North Africa who conquered much of the Peninsula in 711

### Afonso Henriques and the Battle of São Mamede (1128)

The **County of Portucale** was created by the kings of León to defend their northern frontier. Count **Afonso Henriques** wanted to make his county independent.

In **1128**, at the **Battle of São Mamede** (near Guimarães), Afonso Henriques defeated the forces of his own mother, Countess Teresa, and became sole ruler of Portugal.

In **1139**, after victory at the **Battle of Ourique** against the Moors, Afonso Henriques proclaimed himself **King of Portugal**.

In **1143**, the **Treaty of Zamora** formally recognised Portugal's independence.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Guimarães** is known as the **birthplace (cradle) of Portugal** because Afonso Henriques was born there and it was where he began his fight for independence.

---

## The Christian Reconquest

After founding Portugal, Afonso Henriques continued to conquer territory from the Moors southwards. In **1147** he captured **Lisbon**. The last Moorish stronghold, the Algarve, was conquered in **1249** during the reign of **Afonso III**. Portugal's borders were now almost exactly as they are today!

---

## The Portuguese Age of Discoveries

In the 15th century, Portugal led the great **Age of Maritime Discoveries**. The Portuguese wanted to find a sea route to India and explore new lands.

### Key Figures

| Person | Achievement | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Prince Henry the Navigator** | Organised and funded early explorations — School of Sagres | 15th century |
| **Bartolomeu Dias** | Rounded the Cape of Good Hope (southern Africa) | 1488 |
| **Vasco da Gama** | Reached India by sea (the Cape Route) | 1498 |
| **Pedro Álvares Cabral** | Discovered Brazil | 1500 |
| **Ferdinand Magellan** | Led the first circumnavigation of the globe | 1519–1522 |

> [!NOTE]
> **Vasco da Gama** left Lisbon in 1497 with 4 ships. In 1498 he arrived at **Calicut**, in India, opening a trade route that would make Portugal enormously wealthy.

---

## The Iberian Union (1580–1640) 🇪🇸

In **1580**, the Portuguese king died without heirs and the King of Spain, **Philip II**, claimed the throne. For **60 years**, Portugal and Spain shared the **same king**. Portugal lost much influence and several overseas territories during this period.

---

## The Restoration of Independence (1640) 🇵🇹

On **1 December 1640**, a group of Portuguese nobles revolted and expelled the Spanish. **John IV** became the new King of Portugal.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **1 December** is a national holiday — it celebrates the **Restoration of Independence**.

---

## The 1755 Earthquake

On **1 November 1755**, a devastating **earthquake** destroyed much of **Lisbon**. It was followed by a **tsunami** and massive **fires**. Thousands of people died.

The **Marquis of Pombal**, minister to King José I, led the rebuilding of Lisbon with wide streets and stronger buildings — the **Baixa Pombalina** district that still stands today.

---

## The Republic (1910)

On **5 October 1910**, a revolution ended the monarchy in Portugal. The last king, **Manuel II**, went into exile. Portugal became a **Republic** — power now belonged to the people, who elect their representatives.

> [!NOTE]
> **5 October** is a national holiday — Republic Day.

---

## The 25th of April 1974 — The Carnation Revolution

From 1933, Portugal lived under a **dictatorship** (the Estado Novo, led by Salazar and later Caetano). There was no freedom of speech, elections were not free, and Portugal was fighting colonial wars in Africa.

On **25 April 1974**, a group of military officers staged a peaceful revolution. People took to the streets and placed **carnations** in the soldiers' rifle barrels — that's why it's called the **Carnation Revolution**.

After the 25th of April:
- 🗳️ **Free elections** returned
- 📰 **Freedom of the press** was restored
- 🏫 Education expanded to all
- 🌍 African colonies gained independence

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **25 April** is **Freedom Day** — one of Portugal's most important national holidays!

---

## Portugal Today 🇵🇹🇪🇺

- **1986** — Portugal joined the **European Community** (now the European Union)
- **1999** — Macau was returned to China (the last overseas territory)
- **2002** — The **Euro (€)** replaced the escudo as currency
- **2004** — Portugal hosted **Euro 2004** football championship
- **2016** — Portugal won **Euro 2016**! ⚽🏆
- **Today** — Portugal is a democracy, a member of the EU and NATO, with about **10 million people**
