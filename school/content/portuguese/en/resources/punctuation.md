# Punctuation Marks

Punctuation marks are like the **traffic rules** of writing! They tell us when to stop, when to speed up and when to ask a question. Without them, texts become confusing. 🚦

---

## Full Stop (.)

The full stop ends a **declarative sentence** — a sentence that states or tells something.

**Rules:**
- Appears at the end of sentences that are not questions or exclamations
- After abbreviations (Dr., Mrs., p.)
- The next word begins with an **uppercase** letter

**Examples:**
> Pedro went to school.
> The rain fell during the night.
> Today is a beautiful day.

---

## Question Mark (?)

Ends an **interrogative sentence** — a sentence that asks a question.

**Rules:**
- Always appears at the end of the question
- The next word begins with an **uppercase** letter

**Examples:**
> Where is my book?
> What is your name?
> How old are you?

> [!TIP]
> Think: if the sentence asks a question, it ends with **?** 🤔

---

## Exclamation Mark (!)

Appears in sentences that express **strong emotion** — surprise, joy, fear, a command or admiration.

**Rules:**
- Ends exclamatory and imperative sentences
- The next word begins with an **uppercase** letter
- Can appear together with the question mark (?!)

**Examples:**
> What a wonderful day! ☀️
> Watch out, there's a car! 🚗
> Congratulations! 🎉
> Don't come in here!

---

## Comma (,)

The comma makes a **short pause** inside a sentence. It is the most used mark — and one of the most difficult!

**Main uses:**

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| Separate items in a list | I bought bread, milk, eggs and fruit. |
| Isolate a vocative (calling someone) | Ana, come here! |
| Separate clauses | When he got home, he ate straight away. |
| Before "but", "however", "yet" | I wanted to go, but I was tired. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Never put a comma between the subject and the verb!
> ❌ Pedro, went to school.
> ✅ Pedro went to school.

---

## Colon (:)

The colon **announces** something: a list, an explanation, a quotation or direct speech.

**Examples:**
> In my bag I have: notebooks, pencils, an eraser and a ruler. 🎒
> The teacher said: "Open your books at page 10."
> I only need one thing: silence. 🤫

> [!NOTE]
> After a colon, the list or explanation can begin with a lowercase letter, unless it is a direct quotation.

---

## Quotation Marks (" ")

Quotation marks indicate **direct speech** (someone's exact words), titles or special words.

**Examples:**
> Mum said: "Go to sleep now!"
> I read the book "A Carochinha".
> That word is really "cool"!

| Type of marks | Use |
|--------------|-----|
| "text" | Direct speech, titles |
| 'text' | Inside already-open quotation marks |
| «text» | Portuguese typographical style |

---

## — Em Dash (—)

The em dash is longer than the hyphen (-). It has specific uses:

**In dialogues:** shows that a character begins to speak.
> — Good morning! — said the boy.
> — Good morning! — replied the teacher.

**To isolate information:** like more formal parentheses.
> The capital of Portugal — Lisbon — sits by the River Tagus.

> [!TIP]
> The em dash (—) is different from the hyphen (-). The hyphen is shorter and joins words: merry-go-round, father-in-law.

---

## ... Ellipsis (...)

Three dots that create **suspense**, indicate a long pause or show that text has been cut.

**Examples:**
> Once upon a time, in a land far away... 🌙
> I'm not sure... maybe tomorrow.
> The original text says: "There was once a king... who lived in a castle."

> [!NOTE]
> The ellipsis is always **three dots** — not two, not four!

---

## () Parentheses

Enclose **additional information** that is not essential to the sentence.

**Examples:**
> Luís (my best friend) has a birthday tomorrow. 🎂
> The blue whale (the largest animal in the world) can be 30 metres long. 🐋

---

## Summary of All Punctuation Marks

| Mark | Name | Main use |
|:----:|------|----------|
| **.** | Full stop | Ends a declarative sentence |
| **?** | Question mark | Ends a question |
| **!** | Exclamation mark | Emotion, surprise, command |
| **,** | Comma | Short pause, list, vocative |
| **:** | Colon | Before a list or quotation |
| **" "** | Quotation marks | Direct speech, titles |
| **—** | Em dash | Dialogues, extra information |
| **...** | Ellipsis | Suspense, long pause |
| **( )** | Parentheses | Additional information |

> [!TIP]
> When you read aloud, respect the punctuation marks — they tell you when to pause and how to express emotion! 🎭

---

Now you know how to use all the punctuation marks! 🏆 Practise by writing sentences with each one.
