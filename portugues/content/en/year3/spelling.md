# Spelling — Year 3

## Accent Marks (Acentos)

Portuguese uses **accent marks** to show which syllable is stressed and how a vowel is pronounced. There are three main accents:

### Acute Accent ( ´ ) — *Acento Agudo*
Marks an open, stressed vowel.

| Examples | English |
|---------|---------|
| *á**r**vore* | tree |
| *pá* | spade |
| *fácil* | easy |
| *após* | after |

### Circumflex Accent ( ^ ) — *Acento Circunflexo*
Marks a closed, stressed vowel.

| Examples | English |
|---------|---------|
| *avô* | grandfather |
| *vê* | sees |
| *mês* | month |
| *português* | Portuguese |

### Grave Accent ( ` ) — *Acento Grave*
Used only in contractions with the preposition *a* + article *a/as*:

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| *à* (a + a) | to the (f. sg.) |
| *às* (a + as) | to the (f. pl.) |

> [!TIP]
> Words stressed on the **last** syllable (*oxítonas*) that end in -a, -e, -o, -em, -ens always take an accent: *sofá*, *café*, *avó*, *também*.
> Words stressed on the **third-to-last** syllable (*proparoxítonas*) always take an accent: *lâmpada*, *música*, *médico*.

## Digraphs (Dígrafos)

A **digraph** is two letters that together make one sound.

### The Palatal Digraphs

| Digraph | Sound | Examples | English |
|---------|-------|---------|---------|
| **lh** | like the *lli* in *million* | *folha*, *filho*, *palha* | leaf, son, straw |
| **nh** | like the *ny* in *canyon* | *ninho*, *manhã*, *carinho* | nest, morning, affection |
| **ch** | like the *sh* in *shop* | *chave*, *chuva*, *cheio* | key, rain, full |

> [!NOTE]
> **lh** and **nh** are sounds unique to Portuguese — they don't exist in English! Listen carefully and practise them.

### Other Common Digraphs

| Digraph | Sound | Examples |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **rr** | strong *r* (between vowels) | *carro* (car), *terra* (earth) |
| **ss** | *s* sound (between vowels) | *massa* (dough), *passo* (step) |
| **qu** | *k* sound (before *e/i*) | *quero* (I want), *quilo* (kilogram) |
| **gu** | *g* sound (before *e/i*) | *guerra* (war), *guia* (guide) |

## The /s/ Sound — ss, ç, c, s, or z?

The /s/ sound can be spelled several different ways in Portuguese. Here are the rules:

| Spelling | When to use | Examples |
|----------|------------|---------|
| **ss** | between two vowels | *massa*, *passo*, *russo* |
| **ç** | before *a*, *o*, *u* (never at the start of a word) | *açúcar*, *força*, *almoço* |
| **c** | before *e* or *i* | *cidade*, *cedo*, *cinema* |
| **s** | at the start of a word; between consonant and vowel | *sala*, *sol*, *bolsa* |
| **z** | between vowels (in certain words); at the end of a syllable | *fazer*, *buzina*, *rapaz* |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ç** is **never** used before *e* or *i* — use **c** instead: *céu* (sky), *cinto* (belt).

## Common Spelling Mistakes to Avoid

| Incorrect | Correct | Rule |
|-----------|---------|------|
| ~~*paçear*~~ | *passear* | ss between vowels |
| ~~*nasção*~~ | *nação* | ç before a/o/u |
| ~~*reseber*~~ | *receber* | c before e |
| ~~*orizem*~~ | *origem* | origin-based spelling |
