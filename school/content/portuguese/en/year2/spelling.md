# Spelling — Year 2

You already know the letters and accents! Now let's learn the more advanced rules of Portuguese spelling. 💪

---

## Digraphs — Two Letters, One Sound

A **digraph** is when two letters together make just **one sound**. It's like a team! 🤝

| Digraph | Sound | Example | Image |
|:-------:|-------|---------|-------|
| **ch** | sh (like "sheep") | chave (key), chuva (rain) | 🔑 ☔ |
| **lh** | ly (like "million") | folha (leaf), milho (corn) | 🍃 🌽 |
| **nh** | ny (like "canyon") | ninho (nest), banho (bath) | 🐣 🛁 |
| **ss** | s (strong) | passo (step), osso (bone) | 🦴 |
| **rr** | r (strong, rolling) | carro (car), terra (earth) | 🚗 🌍 |
| **qu** | k (before e/i) | queijo (cheese), quilo (kilo) | 🧀 |
| **gu** | g (before e/i) | guerra (war), guitarra (guitar) | 🎸 |

> [!NOTE]
> In the digraphs **qu** and **gu**, the **u** is usually silent when followed by **e** or **i**.
> - que**i**jo → said "kay-jo"
> - guer**r**a → said "gue-rra"

## S, SS, Ç, C — Which One to Use?

This is one of the biggest challenges in Portuguese spelling! Let's look at the rules. 🧩

### When to use **S**?

- At the start of words: **s**ala (room), **s**ol (sun), **s**opa (soup) 🍲
- Between a vowel and a consonant: in**s**ect, per**s**on
- In many common words: **s**apo (frog), **s**orriso (smile) 😊

### When to use **SS**?

- Between two vowels to make a strong S sound:
  - pa**ss**o (step), o**ss**o (bone), ma**ss**a (dough) 🍝

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SS** never appears at the start of a word and never after a consonant!

### When to use **Ç**?

- The cedilla makes the S sound but appears before **a**, **o**, **u**:
  - **ç**a: maça (apple), calça (trousers) 👖
  - **ç**o: almoço (lunch), poço (well)
  - **ç**u: açúcar (sugar) 🍬

### When to use **C**?

- The C makes the S sound before **e** and **i**:
  - **c**e: cebola (onion) 🧅, cereja (cherry) 🍒
  - **c**i: cidade (city), cinema (cinema) 🎬

| S sound | When | Example |
|:-------:|------|---------|
| **s** | start of word | sapo 🐸 |
| **ss** | between vowels | passo |
| **ç** | before a, o, u | açúcar |
| **c** | before e, i | cebola |

## Words with Silent H

The **H** at the start of some words is not pronounced — it is "silent"! But we still have to write it.

| Word | Word | Word |
|------|------|------|
| **h**omem (man) 👨 | **h**ora (hour) ⏰ | **h**ospital 🏥 |
| **h**oje (today) | **h**erói (hero) 🦸 | **h**istória (story) 📖 |
| **h**elicóptero 🚁 | **h**ábito (habit) | **h**igiene (hygiene) 🪥 |

> [!TIP]
> Learn these words by heart, because there's no simple rule — you really do need to memorise them!

> [!NOTE]
> H also appears in the digraphs **ch**, **lh** and **nh**, but there it is part of the digraph and works differently.

## Basic Punctuation Marks

Punctuation marks are like traffic lights for text — they tell us when to stop, question or exclaim!

### The Full Stop (.)

Ends a sentence that states something.

> Rui went to school. 🏫

### The Question Mark (?)

Ends a question.

> Where is my book? 📚

### The Exclamation Mark (!)

Shows surprise, happiness or gives a command.

> What a delicious cake! 🎂
> Be careful! ⚠️

### The Comma (,)

Makes a short pause inside a sentence.

> I bought apples, pears and bananas. 🍎🍐🍌

### The Colon (:)

Introduces a list or an explanation.

> In my bag I have: notebooks, pencils and an eraser. 🎒

### The Ellipsis (...)

Shows that text is unfinished or creates suspense.

> Once upon a time... 🌙

| Mark | Name | Use |
|:----:|------|-----|
| . | Full stop | Ends a sentence |
| ? | Question mark | Asks a question |
| ! | Exclamation mark | Surprise/command |
| , | Comma | Short pause |
| : | Colon | Before a list |
| ... | Ellipsis | Suspense/pause |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> After a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark, the next word always starts with an **uppercase** letter!
