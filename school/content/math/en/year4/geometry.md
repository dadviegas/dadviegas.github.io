# Geometry — Year 4

Welcome to Year 4 Geometry — this is where we get really precise! We'll use a **protractor** to measure angles, calculate **areas**, discover **volume**, and explore the magical world of **circles**! 🔵

---

## Measuring Angles with a Protractor

A **protractor** is a tool for measuring angles in **degrees (°)**.

### How to Use a Protractor

1. Place the **centre hole** exactly on the **vertex** of the angle
2. Line up the **zero line** with one arm of the angle
3. Read the number where the **other arm** crosses the scale
4. That's your angle in degrees! 🎯

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Protractors have **two scales** (inner and outer). Always check which one to use! Start from 0° — if the angle opens to the **right**, use the top scale. If it opens to the **left**, use the bottom scale. 📐

### Angle Summary

| Type | Degrees |
|------|---------|
| Acute | $0° < \text{angle} < 90°$ |
| Right | Exactly $90°$ |
| Obtuse | $90° < \text{angle} < 180°$ |
| Straight | Exactly $180°$ |
| Reflex | $180° < \text{angle} < 360°$ |
| Full turn | $360°$ |

$$\text{Angles on a straight line add up to } 180°$$

$$\text{Angles around a point add up to } 360°$$

## Area — How Much Space?

**Area** is the amount of **surface** inside a 2D shape. We measure area in **square units** (cm², m²).

> [!NOTE]
> Think of area as counting how many **square tiles** would cover a shape perfectly! 🟦

### Area of a Rectangle

$$\text{Area} = \text{length} \times \text{width}$$

$$A = l \times w$$

**Example:** Rectangle with length 8 cm and width 5 cm:

$$A = 8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$$

### Area of a Square

$$\text{Area} = \text{side} \times \text{side} = \text{side}^2$$

**Example:** Square with side 6 cm:

$$A = 6 \times 6 = 36 \text{ cm}^2$$

### Area of a Triangle

$$\text{Area} = \frac{\text{base} \times \text{height}}{2}$$

$$A = \frac{b \times h}{2}$$

**Example:** Triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm:

$$A = \frac{10 \times 6}{2} = \frac{60}{2} = 30 \text{ cm}^2$$

> [!TIP]
> A triangle is exactly **half** of a rectangle with the same base and height! That's why we divide by 2. ✂️

## Volume — How Much Space Inside?

**Volume** is the amount of **3D space** inside a solid shape. We measure volume in **cubic units** (cm³).

> [!NOTE]
> Think of volume as counting how many **small cubes** would fill a box completely! 🎲

$$\text{Volume of a cuboid} = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$$

$$V = l \times w \times h$$

**Example:** Box with length 4 cm, width 3 cm, height 2 cm:

$$V = 4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24 \text{ cm}^3$$

| Shape | Area | Volume |
|-------|------|--------|
| Flat shapes | cm², m² | — |
| 3D solid shapes | — | cm³, m³ |

## Circles — The Perfect Shape ⭕

A **circle** is a perfectly round shape where every point is the **same distance** from the centre.

### Parts of a Circle

```
         ←──── diameter ────→
              ___
           /       \
          │    ★    │
          │    ↑    │
           \   r   /
              ‾‾‾
              (★ = centre, r = radius)
```

| Part | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Centre** | The middle point of the circle |
| **Radius (r)** | Distance from centre to edge |
| **Diameter (d)** | Distance across the circle through the centre |
| **Circumference** | The distance all the way around the circle |

$$\text{diameter} = 2 \times \text{radius}$$

$$d = 2r$$

$$\text{radius} = \frac{\text{diameter}}{2}$$

$$r = \frac{d}{2}$$

**Example:** A circle with radius 5 cm has a diameter of:
$$d = 2 \times 5 = 10 \text{ cm}$$

> [!TIP]
> The **radius** is like the spoke of a wheel — going from the centre to the rim. The **diameter** goes all the way across! 🚲

## Area vs Perimeter — Don't Mix Them Up!

| Measure | What it is | Units |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Perimeter** | Distance around the outside | cm, m |
| **Area** | Space inside the shape | cm², m² |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Perimeter is measured in **single units** (cm, m). Area is measured in **square units** (cm², m²). They are completely different things! 📏📦

## Try It!

1. **Protractor Practice**: Draw 3 angles — one acute (about 45°), one right angle (90°), and one obtuse (about 130°). Measure each one with a protractor. How close were you? 📐

2. **Area of your book**: Measure the length and width of your exercise book in cm. Calculate its **area** in cm²!

3. **Triangle halves**: Draw a rectangle 10 cm × 6 cm. Calculate its area. Now draw a diagonal and calculate the triangle's area. Is it exactly half? 🔺

4. **Circle explore**: Find a circular object (cup, plate, tin). Measure its **diameter** with a ruler. What is the **radius**?

5. **Volume builder**: Stack 12 small cubes (like sugar cubes or building blocks). Arrange them as different cuboids (e.g. 3×2×2, 6×2×1). Do they all have the same volume? 🎲
