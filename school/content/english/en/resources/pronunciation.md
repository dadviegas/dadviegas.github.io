# Pronunciation Guide

## Introduction

English pronunciation can feel unpredictable — the same letters can make different sounds, and words are not always said the way they look. This guide explains the key patterns so you can read and speak English with more confidence.

---

## The Phonemic Alphabet (IPA)

The **International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)** uses symbols to represent sounds consistently. Each symbol always represents the same sound, regardless of spelling.

### Vowel Sounds

| IPA | Example words | Description |
|-----|--------------|-------------|
| /iː/ | **ee**l, s**ee**, b**ea**ch | long "ee" |
| /ɪ/ | **i**t, s**i**t, f**i**sh | short "i" |
| /e/ | **e**gg, b**e**d, h**e**ad | short "e" |
| /æ/ | **a**t, c**a**t, m**a**n | short "a" |
| /ɑː/ | **ar**m, c**ar**, f**a**ther | long "ah" |
| /ɒ/ | **o**n, d**o**g, w**a**tch | short "o" |
| /ɔː/ | **or**, d**oor**, c**au**se | long "aw" |
| /ʊ/ | p**u**t, f**oo**t, c**ou**ld | short "oo" |
| /uː/ | m**oo**n, bl**ue**, s**oo**n | long "oo" |
| /ʌ/ | b**u**t, c**u**p, s**u**n | short "u" (like "uh") |
| /ɜː/ | b**ir**d, h**er**, t**ur**n | "er" sound |
| /ə/ | **a**bout, sist**e**r | schwa (most common!) |

## The Schwa /ə/ — The Most Important Sound!

The **schwa** is the most common sound in English. It appears in unstressed syllables and sounds like a very short, neutral "uh".

| Word | Schwa position | Pronunciation |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| **a**bout | first syllable | /ə'baʊt/ |
| sist**er** | second syllable | /'sɪstə/ |
| **the** (before consonant) | full word | /ðə/ |
| **a** (article) | full word | /ə/ |
| teach**er** | second syllable | /'tiːtʃə/ |

> [!TIP]
> Understanding the schwa explains why English words often sound different from how they're written. The unstressed syllables become short and neutral!

## Common Spelling-Sound Patterns

### The letter A

| Pattern | Sound | Examples |
|---------|-------|---------|
| a + consonant + e | /eɪ/ | c**a**ke, m**a**ke, t**a**ke |
| a (short) | /æ/ | c**a**t, m**a**n, h**a**t |
| a + r | /ɑː/ | c**ar**, f**ar**, st**ar** |
| a (unstressed) | /ə/ | **a**bout, bal**a**nce |

### The letter E

| Pattern | Sound | Examples |
|---------|-------|---------|
| ee | /iː/ | s**ee**, tr**ee**, f**ee**l |
| e + consonant + e | /iː/ | th**e**s**e**, h**e**r**e** |
| e (short) | /e/ | b**e**d, r**e**d, l**e**g |
| ea | /iː/ or /e/ | b**ea**ch / h**ea**d |

### The letter O

| Pattern | Sound | Examples |
|---------|-------|---------|
| o + consonant + e | /əʊ/ | h**o**m**e**, n**o**t**e** |
| oo | /uː/ or /ʊ/ | m**oo**n / f**oo**t |
| o (short) | /ɒ/ | d**o**g, t**o**p |
| ow | /əʊ/ or /aʊ/ | sn**ow** / n**ow** |

## Stressed Syllables

English words have one **stressed** (stronger) syllable. Getting stress right is crucial for being understood!

| Word | Stress | Pronunciation |
|------|--------|---------------|
| **photo** | PHO-to | /'fəʊtəʊ/ |
| **banana** | ba-NA-na | /bə'nɑːnə/ |
| **important** | im-POR-tant | /ɪm'pɔːtənt/ |
| **understand** | un-der-STAND | /ˌʌndə'stænd/ |
| **comfortable** | COM-for-ta-ble | /'kʌmftəbl/ |

> [!TIP]
> Stress can change the meaning of the same word! **RE**cord (noun) vs re**CORD** (verb). **PRE**sent (noun/adjective) vs pre**SENT** (verb).

## The TH Sound — Two Versions!

The letters **TH** make two different sounds in English:

| Sound | IPA | Examples |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Voiceless (like a whispered "t+s") | /θ/ | **th**ink, **th**ree, ba**th**, ma**th** |
| Voiced (tongue between teeth, buzzing) | /ð/ | **th**is, **th**at, **th**e, wi**th** |

To make both: put your tongue between your upper and lower teeth and blow air.
- /θ/ → no voice (just air)
- /ð/ → add voice (buzzing)

## British vs American Pronunciation

| Word | British | American |
|------|---------|---------|
| **water** | WAH-ter (/ˈwɔːtə/) | WAD-er (/ˈwɑːɾər/) |
| **dance** | dahns (/dɑːns/) | dans (/dæns/) |
| **tomato** | tə-MAH-toh | tə-MAY-toh |
| **schedule** | SHED-yool | SKED-jool |
| **advertisement** | ad-VER-tis-ment | ad-ver-TIZE-ment |
| **can't** | KAHNT (/kɑːnt/) | KANT (/kænt/) |
