# Roman Numerals

Roman numerals were created by the ancient Romans over 2,000 years ago. We still use them today! 🏟️

## Rules for Writing Roman Numerals

### The Addition Rule

When a symbol of **smaller** value appears **after** a larger one, you **add** it:

$$VI = 5 + 1 = 6$$
$$XI = 10 + 1 = 11$$
$$LX = 50 + 10 = 60$$
$$MC = 1000 + 100 = 1100$$

### The Subtraction Rule

When a symbol of **smaller** value appears **before** a larger one, you **subtract** it:

$$IV = 5 - 1 = 4$$
$$IX = 10 - 1 = 9$$
$$XL = 50 - 10 = 40$$
$$XC = 100 - 10 = 90$$
$$CD = 500 - 100 = 400$$
$$CM = 1000 - 100 = 900$$

### Important Rules

- The same symbol **cannot be repeated more than 3 times** in a row
- Only **I, X, C, M** can be subtractive (never V, L, D)
- **I** can only subtract **V** and **X**
- **X** can only subtract **L** and **C**
- **C** can only subtract **D** and **M**

## How to Convert

**Roman → Arabic:** Read left to right. If the next symbol is **larger**, subtract the current one; if **equal or smaller**, add it.

**Example:** $XLVII$
$$= XL + V + II = 40 + 5 + 2 = 47$$

**Arabic → Roman:** Break the number into thousands, hundreds, tens and units, then convert each part.

**Example:** $1{,}847$
$$1000 = M \quad 800 = DCCC \quad 40 = XL \quad 7 = VII$$
$$1847 = MDCCCXLVII$$
