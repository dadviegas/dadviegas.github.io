# Telling the Time

## Introduction

Telling the time is an essential everyday skill. In English there are two main ways to express the time — the 12-hour clock (am/pm) and the 24-hour clock. Let's master both!

---

## The 12-Hour Clock

| Expression | Portuguese | Example |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| **o'clock** | em ponto | It's three **o'clock**. |
| **half past** | e meia | It's **half past** four. (4:30) |
| **quarter past** | e um quarto | It's **quarter past** two. (2:15) |
| **quarter to** | menos um quarto | It's **quarter to** six. (5:45) |
| **past** | e | It's twenty **past** three. (3:20) |
| **to** | para | It's ten **to** seven. (6:50) |

> [!TIP]
> Use **past** for the first 30 minutes (1–30) and **to** for the last 30 minutes (31–59).
> - 3:10 → ten **past** three
> - 3:50 → ten **to** four

## AM and PM

| Period | Hours | Portuguese |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| **am** (ante meridiem) | midnight to noon | da manhã |
| **pm** (post meridiem) | noon to midnight | da tarde / da noite |

Examples:
- **7:00 am** → seven o'clock in the morning
- **2:30 pm** → half past two in the afternoon
- **10:00 pm** → ten o'clock at night

---

## Asking and Telling the Time

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **What time is it?** | It's half past three. |
| **What's the time?** | It's quarter to eight. |
| **Have you got the time?** | Yes — it's five past ten. |
| **What time does … start?** | It starts at half past one. |
| **What time does … finish?** | It finishes at three o'clock. |

## The 24-Hour Clock

Used in timetables, transport, and formal contexts:

| 24-hour | 12-hour |
|---------|---------|
| 08:00 | 8 am (eight o'clock in the morning) |
| 12:00 | noon / midday |
| 14:30 | 2:30 pm (half past two in the afternoon) |
| 17:45 | 5:45 pm (quarter to six) |
| 20:00 | 8 pm (eight o'clock in the evening) |
| 23:15 | 11:15 pm (quarter past eleven at night) |
