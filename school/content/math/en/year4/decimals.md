# Decimal Numbers

**Decimal numbers** allow us to represent parts of a whole number. We use a **decimal point** to separate the whole part from the decimal part.

$$3.7 \quad = \quad 3 \text{ ones} + 7 \text{ tenths}$$

---

## Tenths and hundredths

| Fraction         | Decimal | Read as              |
|------------------|---------|----------------------|
| $\frac{1}{10}$   | $0.1$   | zero point one       |
| $\frac{3}{10}$   | $0.3$   | zero point three     |
| $\frac{1}{100}$  | $0.01$  | zero point zero one  |
| $\frac{25}{100}$ | $0.25$  | zero point two five  |

---

## Place value with decimals

In the number **12.47**:

| Position    | Digit | Value  |
|-------------|-------|--------|
| Tens        | 1     | $10$   |
| Ones        | 2     | $2$    |
| Tenths      | 4     | $0.4$  |
| Hundredths  | 7     | $0.07$ |

$$12.47 = 10 + 2 + 0.4 + 0.07$$

---

## Adding decimals

Line up the **decimal points** and add normally:

```
  3.4
+ 1.8
-----
  5.2
```

Another example:

```
  12.35
+  4.70
-------
  17.05
```

> [!TIP]
> Always align the **decimal points** — not just the digits!

---

## Subtracting decimals

```
  8.6
- 3.9
-----
  4.7
```

---

## Decimals in real life

- A bottle holds $1.5$ litres of water.
- A pencil is $17.3$ cm long.
- A bag of rice weighs $0.5$ kg.
