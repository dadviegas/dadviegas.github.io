# Fractions

A **fraction** represents a **part of a whole**.

$$\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$$

- The **denominator** tells us how many equal parts the whole is divided into.
- The **numerator** tells us how many parts we are considering.

---

## Common fractions

### Half — $\dfrac{1}{2}$

Divide into **2 equal parts**, take **1**.

$$\frac{1}{2} \text{ of } 8 = 4$$

### Third — $\dfrac{1}{3}$

Divide into **3 equal parts**, take **1**.

$$\frac{1}{3} \text{ of } 12 = 4$$

### Quarter — $\dfrac{1}{4}$

Divide into **4 equal parts**, take **1**.

$$\frac{1}{4} \text{ of } 20 = 5$$

---

## Reading fractions

| Fraction        | Read as           |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| $\frac{1}{2}$   | one half          |
| $\frac{1}{3}$   | one third         |
| $\frac{1}{4}$   | one quarter       |
| $\frac{2}{3}$   | two thirds        |
| $\frac{3}{4}$   | three quarters    |
| $\frac{5}{8}$   | five eighths      |

---

## Equivalent fractions

Different fractions can represent the **same amount**:

$$\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}$$

> [!NOTE]
> These are **equivalent** because they represent the same portion — only the way of dividing changes.

---

## Comparing fractions with the same denominator

When denominators are equal, compare the **numerators**:

$$\frac{3}{8} < \frac{5}{8} \qquad \text{(3 parts is less than 5 parts)}$$
