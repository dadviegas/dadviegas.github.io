# Geometry — Year 3

This year we're going deeper into shapes! We'll learn about **angles**, measure **perimeters**, and explore **polygons** from triangles all the way to hexagons! 🚀

---

## What is an Angle?

An **angle** is the amount of **turn** between two lines that meet at a point. Think of opening a door — the more you open it, the bigger the angle! 🚪

The point where the two lines meet is called the **vertex** of the angle.

$$\text{A full turn} = 360°$$

$$\text{A half turn} = 180°$$

$$\text{A quarter turn} = 90°$$

---

## Types of Angles

| Angle Type | Size | How to Remember |
|------------|------|-----------------|
| **Right angle** | Exactly 90° | The corner of a square! ◻️ |
| **Acute angle** | Less than 90° | Small and *a-cute* little angle! 🐭 |
| **Obtuse angle** | Between 90° and 180° | Large and wide, like a big yawn 🥱 |

```
Right angle:      Acute angle:    Obtuse angle:
    |                /               \
    |___          /               ____\
   (= 90°)    (< 90°)           (> 90°)
```

> [!TIP]
> Use the **corner of a piece of paper** to check for right angles — it's a perfect 90°! If the angle is smaller than the corner, it's acute. If it's bigger, it's obtuse! 📄

> [!NOTE]
> The little **square symbol** (□) drawn in a corner means it is a **right angle** of exactly 90°. You'll see this on diagrams all the time! 📐

## Polygons — Shapes with Straight Sides

A **polygon** is any **closed shape** with **straight sides**. The name comes from how many sides it has:

| Name | Sides | Angles | Example |
|------|-------|--------|---------|
| **Triangle** | 3 | 3 | 🔺 Traffic sign |
| **Quadrilateral** | 4 | 4 | 🟦 Window |
| **Pentagon** | 5 | 5 | 🏛️ Pentagon building |
| **Hexagon** | 6 | 6 | 🐝 Honeycomb cell |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In a polygon, the **number of sides** always equals the **number of angles**! A triangle has 3 sides AND 3 angles. A hexagon has 6 sides AND 6 angles. Always! 🔁

### Regular vs Irregular Polygons

- A **regular polygon** has all sides **equal** AND all angles **equal** (e.g. a square, an equilateral triangle)
- An **irregular polygon** has sides or angles of **different sizes** (e.g. a scalene triangle)

---

## Classifying Triangles

Triangles can be sorted in two ways — by their **sides** or by their **angles**:

### By Sides

| Type | Description | Sides |
|------|-------------|-------|
| **Equilateral** | All 3 sides equal | All equal ✅✅✅ |
| **Isosceles** | 2 sides equal | Two equal ✅✅❌ |
| **Scalene** | No sides equal | None equal ❌❌❌ |

### By Angles

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Right-angled** | Has one 90° angle |
| **Acute** | All angles less than 90° |
| **Obtuse** | Has one angle greater than 90° |

$$\text{Sum of angles in any triangle} = 180°$$

> [!TIP]
> Tear off the three corners of any triangle and put them together — they always form a **straight line** (180°)! Try it! ✂️

## Perimeter

The **perimeter** is the **total distance around** the outside of a shape. Think of it as walking all the way around the edge!

$$\text{Perimeter} = \text{sum of all sides}$$

### Rectangle Perimeter

$$P = \text{length} + \text{width} + \text{length} + \text{width}$$

$$P = 2 \times \text{length} + 2 \times \text{width}$$

**Example:** Rectangle with length 6 cm and width 4 cm:

$$P = 2 \times 6 + 2 \times 4 = 12 + 8 = 20 \text{ cm}$$

### Square Perimeter

$$P = 4 \times \text{side}$$

**Example:** Square with side 5 cm:

$$P = 4 \times 5 = 20 \text{ cm}$$

> [!NOTE]
> Perimeter is always measured in **length units** like cm or m — because it's a distance! 📏

## Finding Angles in Shapes

| Shape | Right Angles? |
|-------|---------------|
| Square | 4 right angles |
| Rectangle | 4 right angles |
| Equilateral triangle | None! All are acute |
| Right-angled triangle | Exactly 1 right angle |

## Try It!

1. **Angle Hunt**: Go around your classroom and find **3 right angles**, **2 acute angles**, and **2 obtuse angles**. Draw each one and label it!

2. **Triangle Tear**: Draw a triangle, cut it out, then tear off all three corners. Place the corners together at a point — what shape do they make? (Hint: it's a straight line = 180°!) 🔺

3. **Perimeter Walk**: Measure your classroom floor (ask your teacher!). What is the **perimeter** of the room in metres?

4. **Polygon Count**: Draw a hexagon. How many **triangles** can you see inside it if you draw lines from the centre to each corner? (Hint: 6!)

5. **Triangle Classifier**: Draw three different triangles. Classify each one by its sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) AND by its angles (acute, right-angled, obtuse)!
